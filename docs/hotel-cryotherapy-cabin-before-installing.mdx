**Short answer:** when Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions plans a cryotherapy room for a hotel, 7 things are settled before the cabin arrives: where the cabin sits in the spa, the guest route to and from it, a staff station that can see it, the cabin type (electric or nitrogen), the power supply and heat rejection, the delivery route and hours, and who services it. The cabin itself is made by its maker and comes ready; the hotel's work is the room, the route and the people around it.

## Where in the spa should the cabin go?

Near the changing rooms and the rest area, on the dry side of the spa, away from the steam room and the hammam. Guests use the cabin for a short time and move on, so it fits well on the same floor as the sauna and cold plunge, where the guest route is short. The room needs a closed door, a waiting place and enough space around the cabin for the clearances its maker asks for.

## What does the guest route need?

A short, dry path from the changing room, a place to leave a robe and slippers, and a return to the rest area without crossing wet zones. In a hotel the cabin is often booked in short slots; a waiting chair outside the room and a clear sign of when the cabin is in use keep the route calm.

## Why does the cabin need a staff station?

Because a staff member runs each session from the cabin's control and watches the guest. The station needs a direct view of the cabin, the control within reach and space for a small desk. Plan it with the room, not after; a station squeezed into a corner is hard to fix once the room is finished.

## Electric or nitrogen: what changes for a hotel?

An electric cabin, such as CryoOne in our range, needs a dedicated power supply and a way to remove the heat its refrigeration gives off; it needs no gas deliveries. A nitrogen cabin needs a storage vessel, a regular delivery route for refills, an exhaust for the gas and oxygen monitoring in the room, as its maker sets out. For a hotel, the daily side matters: a nitrogen cabin brings a supplier and a delivery schedule into the back of house.

## How does a cabin fit with other cold rooms?

Many hotel spas already have a cold zone: a cold plunge, a snow room like the one above, an ice fountain or a shock shower. A cabin added to that zone shares the guest route and, often, the technical room behind it. The visit checks whether the existing plant room has space and air for another heat-rejecting unit, or whether the cabin needs its own.

## What about delivery and installation in a working hotel?

The route from the loading bay to the spa is measured against the cabin's delivery dimensions, lifts are booked, floors on the route are protected and the work is planned for quiet hours. The maker's or supplier's technician connects the cabin and runs a test cycle. Our [cryotherapy](https://saunadekor.com/cryotherapy/) page covers the cabins and the room work we prepare.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a hotel need a technical room for a cryotherapy cabin?

An electric cabin gives off heat through its refrigeration. That heat goes into the cabin room or to a separate technical space or outdoor unit, depending on the model. The room's ventilation is sized for it.

### Who makes the cabin and who installs it?

The cabin and its refrigeration are made by the maker, who owns its documents and factory warranty. We select and supply the cabin, prepare the room, the power line and the ventilation, and bring the cabin into service with the maker's technician.

### Can the cabin go next to the steam room?

It is better kept on the dry side of the spa. Humid air from a steam room or a hammam is not good for the cabin's electronics or its room climate. The visit records the neighbours of the room.

### How long does a hotel need to prepare the room?

It depends on the building. A room with power nearby and an easy delivery route is quick; a new power line, a long route or a nitrogen supply takes longer. The site visit report sets the sequence.

### What should be in the service agreement?

Planned maintenance visits, response times, spare parts and who replaces filters. The cabin maker's manual lists the routine checks; the agreement says who does them.
