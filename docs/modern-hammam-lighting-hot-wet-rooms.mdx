**Short answer:** a modern hammam is lit mostly with lines, not points: sealed linear light in shadow gaps under the benches and along the walls, a few small downlights kept away from the heated platform, and sometimes a backlit stone panel as the one bright feature. Polished large slabs reflect every fitting, so positions are drawn against the stone layout. Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions sets the lighting plan together with the slab drawings, because the light lines run in joints that are cut at the workshop.

## What makes a modern hammam harder to light?

Three things. The surfaces are large and often polished, so a downlight shows twice: once in the ceiling and once in the floor or wall. The design has few visible fittings, so the light has to hide in joints, gaps and recesses. And people lie on the heated platform looking straight up, so the ceiling is seen more than in any other room.

## Where should the light come from?

| Light | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Linear light under the bench | Recessed profile in a shadow gap under the bench lip | Lights the floor and shows the bench edge without glare |
| Linear light at wall level | A slot above the backrest or in a vertical joint | Washes the slab and shows its veins |
| Downlights | Over the walkways and basins, not over the platform | Light where people walk and wash |
| Backlit panel | One wall, behind translucent stone such as onyx | A single feature, on its own scene |
| Step and drain light | Low, at steps and linear drains | Shows level changes on a wet floor |

## Why keep fittings away from the platform?

Because someone lying on the heated platform looks straight into anything above it. Downlights over the platform also mirror in its polished top. We light the platform from its edges, with a line under its lip or a wash from the walls, and keep the ceiling above it plain or with very small fibre-optic points only.

## How are light lines built into stone?

In a joint planned on the slab drawing. The stone maker cuts a recess or leaves a shadow gap; a sealed aluminium or silicone profile is fixed in it, and its cable runs in a duct behind the waterproofing to the outside of the room. The profile's rating for moisture and ambient temperature comes from its maker's datasheet. Lines are kept short enough that each one can be replaced from inside the room without lifting a slab.

## Can onyx or other stone be lit from behind?

Yes, and it is often the one feature of a modern hammam. The panel needs a sealed, ventilated cavity behind it, with the LED panel and the stone held apart at an even distance so the light has no hot spots. The cavity is waterproofed like the rest of the wall, its driver sits outside the room, and the stone is fixed so it can be taken off to reach the light. Translucent stone is chosen from the stone maker's data for wet rooms.

## What colour and control suit a modern hammam?

Warm white for most of the room; it keeps grey and white stone from looking cold. Two or three scenes are enough: cleaning at full light, bathing with lines and the backlit panel, and a low setting for the end of the evening. The control sits outside, beside the glass door. A glass door also carries light out into the corridor, so the scene near the door is set with the corridor in mind.

Colour-changing light can be added to the same fittings; we treat it as a design choice only.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do downlights have to be avoided altogether?

No. They work over walkways and basins. They are kept away from the platform and from places where they mirror in polished stone.

### Is microcement easier to light than marble?

Microcement is matt, so it reflects less. Light lines still need planned recesses, because microcement cannot be cut cleanly after it is laid.

### Where do the drivers go?

Outside the room, in a dry and ventilated place with access for service.
