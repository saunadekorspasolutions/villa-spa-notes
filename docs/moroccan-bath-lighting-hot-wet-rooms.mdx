**Short answer:** a Moroccan bath is lit with sealed low-voltage fittings or fibre optics, set into the plaster or stone before the finish goes on, with every driver and light source outside the room. Three kinds of light do the work: soft ambient light from the ceiling or vault, a brighter task light over the scrub bench, and accent light in the niches. Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions fixes the fitting positions on the drawings before plastering, because a fitting added later means cutting a finished lime-plaster wall.

## Why is lighting a Moroccan bath different?

The general rules for any steam room still apply: high moisture, sealed fittings, drivers outside. A Moroccan bath adds three things. Its walls and vault are often lime plaster or tadelakt, which is applied by hand in layers and cannot be patched invisibly. It has a scrub bench where someone works with a kese mitt and black soap, and that work needs good light. And its decoration, from zellige niches to lanterns, invites fittings that were never made for steam.

## Which fittings suit a plastered room?

Fittings with a high ingress protection rating against moisture and water jets, and an ambient temperature rating that covers a steam room. The rating is the IP code defined in IEC 60529; the fitting maker's datasheet states both the IP code and the maximum ambient temperature. We choose from that datasheet for each project, not from a catalogue picture.

In plaster, the fitting sits in a sealed sleeve fixed to the substrate and waterproofed into the membrane before the plaster coats go on. The plaster then finishes against the sleeve's rim. A fitting screwed straight into plaster cracks the finish round its edge within the first weeks of steam.

| Light | Where | What to choose | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ambient | Vault or ceiling, away from the steam outlet | Fibre-optic points or small sealed downlights in sleeves | Fittings directly above the steam outlet |
| Task | Over the scrub bench | A brighter sealed fitting, warm white, aimed at the bench top | Light behind the person scrubbing |
| Accent | Niches, under the bench lip | Low-voltage LED in a sealed silicone profile | Open LED strips and tape |
| Decorative lantern | Outside the room, or in the changing area | A real lantern in the dry zone | Metal lanterns inside the steam |

## Where does the light source go?

Outside the room, always. Drivers, transformers and the fibre-optic light source go in the plant cupboard or a dry service void with an access door. Only cable or glass fibre enters the room, in a sealed duct. How a fibre-optic star ceiling is built is the same as in any steam room; in a Moroccan vault the fibres are set in the substrate before plastering and trimmed flush afterwards.

## How bright should the scrub bench be?

Bright enough to see the skin and the soap, without glare. We place the task light slightly in front of the bench, so the person scrubbing does not shade the bench top, and on its own switch or scene so it can be turned down when the bench is not in use. Warm white suits plaster and zellige; a cold white makes lime plaster look grey.

## Can lit niches and lanterns be used?

Niches, yes: a sealed LED profile hidden in the niche lip lights zellige or a carved panel well, and the driver goes outside with the rest. Lanterns are a different matter. A pierced metal lantern hung in steam corrodes and drips rust onto the plaster. We hang lanterns in the changing area or the rest room, where the style carries through without the moisture.

## How are the lights controlled and cleaned?

Controls sit outside the door: one switch or keypad with two or three scenes, for example cleaning, bathing and a low evening setting. Colour-changing light is possible on the same fittings; we treat it as a design choice, not as a feature with any effect on the bather.

Black soap and clay leave a film on lenses near the bench. Flush lenses with no grooves are wiped with the walls at the end of the day. Fittings with recessed rings collect soap, so we avoid them close to the bench.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can lights be added after the plaster is finished?

They can, but the plaster round each new fitting has to be cut and remade, and the repair usually shows. It is cheaper to decide positions on the drawings.

### Is fibre optic better than LED in a Moroccan bath?

Fibre optic puts no electricity in the room and suits small points in a vault. Sealed LED gives more light for the scrub bench. Most rooms use both.

### Where does the light switch go?

Outside the room, beside the door, with the steam generator control.
