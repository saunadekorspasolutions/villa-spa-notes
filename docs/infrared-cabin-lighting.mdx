**Short answer:** light an infrared cabin with low-voltage LED fittings rated for the cabin temperature, keep every driver and transformer outside the cabin where a technician can reach it, and never place a fitting in front of an emitter panel. Strips behind the backrest or under the bench give enough light to sit by; one soft ceiling point is enough for cleaning. Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions draws the light positions on the same sheet as the emitters so the two never collide.

An infrared cabin runs at about 45–60 °C cabin air and has no water inside. That makes lighting easier than in a Finnish sauna or a steam room, but the panels give off radiant heat, and the glass front turns every bright point into a reflection.

## Which fittings survive inside an infrared cabin?

The fitting must be rated by its maker for the temperature at the spot where it sits, not for the room average. The cabin top is the warmest place, and a fitting a hand's width from an emitter sees much more heat than the cabin air reading suggests.

- **Low-voltage LED strip** in an aluminium profile with a diffuser, fixed mechanically. Adhesive tape alone lets go after a few weeks of heat.
- **Small recessed LED points** in the ceiling, rated for heat, with the cable run above the cabin roof.
- **Fibre optic points** where the owner wants a star ceiling: the light source sits outside, only glass fibre enters the cabin.

Mains-voltage lamps and fittings with a driver built into the body do not go inside.

## Where do the drivers and transformers go?

Outside the cabin, always. Good places are the top of the cabin roof with a lift-off panel, a ventilated cupboard next to the cabin, or the room ceiling above it with an access hatch. Each driver needs air around it and a label saying which strip it feeds.

The usual failure is a driver tucked into the gap between the cabin wall and the room wall. It overheats, the light flickers, and the cabin has to be partly taken apart to reach it.

## Where should the light sit in the cabin?

Light from behind and below the user, not from in front. People in an infrared cabin sit still and look forward, usually through the glass front. A strip behind the backrest lights the timber without shining into anyone's eyes; a strip under the bench lights the floor for getting in and out.

Keep fittings off the emitter faces and off the space directly in front of them. A fitting there blocks part of the panel, takes the heat itself and leaves a shadow on the user.

## What does the glass front change?

At night, glass acts as a mirror. Bright points inside the cabin reflect back at the user, and bright downlights in the room reflect in from outside. Dim the room lights when the cabin is in use, keep cabin fittings low and indirect, and avoid a ceiling spot right above the door.

## How is the light switched?

The simplest set-up switches the cabin light from the cabin controller, so it comes on with the emitters and turns off at the end of the session. A separate low-level setting helps for cleaning with the emitters off. Coloured light is a matter of mood and design; we do not sell it as a treatment.

## What if the cabin is near a shower?

The cabin itself is dry, but in a bathroom the area around it may fall inside a wet zone under local wiring rules. Then the cabin's fittings, their drivers and the switch need a protection rating that suits that zone. The electrician confirms the zone; we choose the fittings to match.

## Fitting check table

| Fitting | Where | What to check | What goes wrong if missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| LED strip | Behind backrest, under bench | Heat rating, aluminium profile, mechanical fixing | Strip peels off, light turns patchy |
| Ceiling point | Cabin ceiling, away from emitters | Heat rating, cable route above roof | Fitting fails early, roof panel has to come off |
| Fibre optic | Star ceiling | Light source outside, service access | Source cooked inside the cabin |
| Driver / transformer | Outside the cabin, ventilated, labelled | Free air, hatch, label | Flicker, dismantling to reach it |
| Room lights | Outside, near the glass | Dimming, no spot above the door | Glare and reflections in the glass |

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use ordinary bathroom downlights inside the cabin?

No. A bathroom downlight is rated for water, not for heat. Inside the cabin the fitting needs a heat rating from its maker for the spot where it sits.

### How bright should the cabin be?

Bright enough to see the bench edge and read a label, no more. Two indirect strips are usually enough; the room lights do the rest.

### Who installs the cabin lighting?

We fit the lights and drivers with the cabin. The electrician brings the circuit and confirms the protection; installation takes about 1 day, and larger models up to 2–3 days.

### Can lights be added to an existing cabin?

Usually, if there is a route for the cable above the roof and a place outside for the driver. Strips behind the backrest are the easiest addition.
