**Short answer:** at Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions we light a snow room with low-voltage LED or fibre-optic fittings that their maker rates for the room's low temperature and for water, keep every driver and transformer on the warm side of the insulation, and seal each cable entry through the wall as carefully as a pipe. Fittings sit on the ceiling line away from the snow outlet, at a low level for footing, or outside the glass front. White snow reflects almost everything, so the light level stays low and soft.

A snow room holds about −10 °C, the value given by its maker TechnoAlpin. It is not hot, but it is wet: snow falls, frost builds, and the room is defrosted and cleaned with water. That makes it one of the hardest rooms in a spa to light.

## Why is a snow room hard to light?

Three things happen together. The cold stiffens seals and housings and can stop a fitting that was made for a warm room. Water reaches the fittings as meltwater during defrost and as spray during cleaning. And at every point where a cable passes through the wall, warm, humid air from outside tries to follow it in and freezes inside the insulation. A light that works on day one can fail in the first season if any of the three is missed.

## Which fittings work at about −10 °C?

Fittings whose maker states, in writing, that they are rated for the low temperature of the room and protected against water and frost. That usually means sealed low-voltage LED fittings, or fibre-optic ends, where the light source sits outside the room and only glass fibre enters it. Fibre optics suit a snow room well, because there is no electrical part inside the cold zone at all. The protection class and temperature range come from the fitting maker's data sheet; we check them against the room before ordering.

## Where do the drivers and cable entries go?

Drivers, transformers and fibre-optic light sources go on the warm side, in a dry, reachable place outside the room. The new point in a snow room is the cable entry itself. Each cable that crosses the wall passes through insulation and the vapour control layer, and that hole is sealed on the warm side with the same care as the refrigeration pipes. An open cable entry is a frost line on the wall within weeks.

## Where should the light sit in the room?

- **Ceiling line:** a track or a few spots, kept clear of the snow outlet so the fall does not bury or ice the fitting.
- **Low level:** small marker lights along the floor edge or under a bench, so people see where to step on a floor that may be slippery.
- **Behind the glass front:** light from the warm side, through the glazing, puts no fitting in the cold at all. The entry zone outside the door is lit the same way.
- **Not on the walls that frost most:** a fitting covered in frost gives patchy light and is hard to reach.

## How does white snow change the light?

Snow and frost reflect light strongly. A level that looks right in a corridor feels harsh in a snow room, and bright points show as glare on every frosted surface and on the glass front. We keep the level low, use dimming, and choose a light colour that the owner sees on a sample before ordering. Coloured scenes are possible; in a snow room they are atmosphere, nothing more.

## How is the lighting switched and serviced?

The switch or scene panel sits outside the room, next to the door. One light stays on whenever someone is inside, so the room is never dark with the door closed. Fittings are reached from the room after it has been brought up to temperature, or from the warm side for fittings behind glass. The lighting circuit is drawn with the refrigeration plant, so both can be isolated for service.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can ordinary bathroom lights go in a snow room?

No. A wet-room rating alone is not enough; the fitting must also be rated for the cold, in writing, by its maker.

### Is fibre optic better than LED in a snow room?

It keeps every electrical part out of the cold zone, which is a clear plus. Sealed cold-rated LED works too, with its driver outside.

### Can the lights melt the snow?

Low-voltage LED gives off little heat. A fitting placed right under the snow outlet can still leave a bare patch, which is why it stays clear of it.

### Can lights be added after handover?

Yes, but each new cable entry has to be sealed through the wall. Fittings behind the glass front are the simplest addition.
