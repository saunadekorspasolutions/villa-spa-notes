**Short answer:** in a hot, humid climate a sauna wall is built in 5 layers from the room outwards: timber panelling, a ventilated gap, a foil vapour barrier, insulation, then the existing wall. The vapour barrier still goes on the room side, because the room at 80–100 °C is the warm side whenever the sauna runs. What changes in the Gulf is the outside: walls in sun, cooled ceiling voids and humid air around the cabin.

Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions designs and builds wet areas for villas and hotels in the Gulf, and the notes below come from that site work.

## Which side does the vapour barrier go on?

The sauna side, behind the panelling and its air gap. When the sauna runs, the room is far warmer than anything around it, so moisture from bathers and from water on the stones is driven outwards. A foil barrier on the room side, with taped joints, stops it from reaching the insulation.

## What about humid air outside the sauna?

When the sauna is off, the room cools and humid outside air can meet the cool shell. The answer is not a second barrier on the outside, which would trap moisture between 2 layers, but a well-sealed room-side barrier, a ventilated gap, and drying the sauna after use by leaving the door open or running the extract.

| Layer, from the room | Purpose | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| Timber panelling | surface people touch | varnish or paint that traps moisture |
| Ventilated gap | lets panelling dry | panels fixed straight onto the foil |
| Foil vapour barrier | keeps moisture out of insulation | untaped joints, torn at sockets |
| Insulation | keeps heat in | gaps at corners and ceiling |
| Existing wall | structure | outside wall in full sun left untreated |

## How are outside walls in sun handled?

An outside wall facing the afternoon sun adds heat and stresses the shell. Where possible, the sauna sits on an internal wall. If not, the insulation on that wall is continuous and the outer face is shaded or reflective.

## Why is the ceiling the weak point?

Heat rises, so the ceiling sees the highest temperatures; above it there is often a cooled void with ducts. The ceiling gets continuous insulation and a carefully taped barrier, with light fittings sealed through it. A gap here lets hot, moist air reach cold ducts, where it condenses.

## What does the room need after use?

Drying. Leave the door open for a while or run the extract so the panelling and gap dry out. Our guide to [sauna insulation](https://saunadekor.com/sauna-insulation/) shows the build-up we use in villas and hotels.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a sauna in the Gulf need a vapour barrier?

Yes, on the room side, as anywhere. The climate changes the outside of the wall, not the side the barrier goes on.

### Can I use a plastic sheet instead of foil?

Use the barrier the heater and panelling makers specify for sauna heat; foil barriers are common because they tolerate the temperature.

### Why leave a gap behind the panelling?

So the timber can dry after each session. Panels fixed straight onto the barrier stay damp.

### Should the outside of the wall be sealed too?

Not with a second vapour barrier, which would trap moisture. Seal the room side well and keep the outside able to dry.

### What is the most common insulation mistake?

Gaps at corners and the ceiling, and barriers torn around cables. See our [sauna insulation](https://saunadekor.com/sauna-insulation/) guide.
